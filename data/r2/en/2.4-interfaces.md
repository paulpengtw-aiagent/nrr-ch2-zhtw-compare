## 2.4 Interfaces

Chapter 3 links to this chapter through the simulated scenarios. At each disruption level, every trace of a shared dependency in Chapter 3 covers each simulated scenario that §§2.1–2.3 list under that level. Each trace presents the simulated scenarios as a nested list, in the form set out in §3.0.3.

Chapter 1 links to this chapter through the Threat context. The report defines a Threat context as a geopolitical or natural condition: a peacetime accident, a blockade (being surrounded), a landing by the People's Liberation Army (PLA), or a mega-quake. The report discusses Threat contexts in Chapter 1, the Background chapter, only.

The report treats Threat contexts as causally separate from the disruption levels: any Threat context might produce any disruption level, or none. These geopolitical and natural-hazard conditions therefore frame the report for the reader in Chapter 1. Chapters 2 and 3 do not use Threat contexts as analytical axes or causal inputs. Chapters 2 and 3 also do not name a Threat context as the cause of a disruption level.
