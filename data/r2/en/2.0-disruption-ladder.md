## 2.0 The disruption ladder

This chapter defines three disruption levels: **50% Degradation** (降載), **Global Cutoff**, and **Island Partition**. The three levels form an escalation ladder: each level includes every effect of the level below it.

Under 50% Degradation, the island loses approximately 50% of its foreign-routed bandwidth, the bandwidth that connects it to the global internet. Global Cutoff takes the conditions of 50% Degradation and removes the remaining foreign-routed bandwidth. Island Partition adds the loss of north–south connectivity inside Taiwan to that total global loss. The domestic network therefore operates normally at the first two levels, and Island Partition is the only level at which traffic between Taiwanese cities fails.

The report anchors its analysis on 50% Degradation. Every trace of a shared dependency in Chapter 3 begins at that level and moves up through Global Cutoff to Island Partition.

The table below shows the expected signal at each level on four observable measures: each column is one level, and each row is one measure. Some cells name a simulated scenario, a narrowed variant of one level, and the roster at the end of this section lists all ten. The evidence behind each cell is in §§2.1–2.3, under Recognition signatures.

The table uses routing terms from the Border Gateway Protocol (BGP), the protocol that networks use to advertise routes to each other. A network cancels a route by sending a BGP WITHDRAW message. Each network is an autonomous system (AS), and the AS path of a route lists the networks that the route crosses. The control plane is the exchange of these routing messages, and the data plane is the forwarding of user traffic.

| | 50% Degradation | Global Cutoff | Island Partition |
|---|---|---|---|
| BGP behavior | During the sustained plateau (the period in which capacity stays low), routers send no WITHDRAW messages: the control plane stays silent while the data plane degrades. At onset, Multi-Cable Loss adds a short spike of WITHDRAW messages before routes reconverge (settle) onto the surviving paths. | Three patterns lead to the same data-plane outcome. First, physical cable loss produces a cliff of WITHDRAW messages (a sudden mass withdrawal of routes). Second, under administrative allowlisting (passing only approved traffic), as in Iran in 2026, routes stay advertised but traffic falls to zero. Third, in a forwarding failure inside a carrier, as at Rogers in 2022, routes stay advertised but traffic falls to zero. | Routes inside each half of the island stay stable, while prefixes (blocks of addresses) in the other half are withdrawn or unreachable. AS paths shift toward a new upstream AS. |
| Traffic plane | Traffic is degraded but survives: round-trip time (RTT) is higher and packet loss is sustained, with no cliff (no sudden drop). The domestic network is intact. | A cliff: international traffic drops to near zero within minutes of onset, while domestic traffic is intact. | Traffic is normal inside each half, and zero between the halves and to the rest of the world. |
| DNS/cache behavior | The rate of SERVFAIL responses rises gradually as resolver RTT exceeds client timeouts. SERVFAIL is the code that a Domain Name System (DNS) resolver returns when it cannot complete a lookup. The cache hit rate may rise, and there is no immediate cliff. | When the cutoff is triggered through BGP, lookups for domains whose authoritative servers are outside Taiwan return SERVFAIL at once. Cached responses then expire in staggered waves, each when its time-to-live (TTL) runs out. Records served through content delivery networks (CDNs) expire after hours, and platform records after 2–3 days. Mail-exchanger (MX) and name-server (NS) records with a long TTL expire after up to 7 days. | Lookups succeed inside each half and fail between the halves. The cache gives a grace period, which shrinks until the TTLs expire. |

For physical loss (All-Cable Loss), the queue for repair ships governs recovery, at weeks per segment. For an administrative order with cables intact (Administrative Cutoff), only a political reversal ends the cutoff, and no engineering schedule applies. Documented cases of this kind range from a shutdown of 7 days ([OONI, November 2019](https://ooni.org/post/2019-iran-internet-blackout/)) to one that April 2026 reporting described as still ongoing and record-breaking ([Al Jazeera, April 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/5/frustration-grows-as-irans-wartime-internet-shutdown-breaks-grim-record/)). §2.2 follows the later case further. | Recovery has two tracks: global restoration and repair of the domestic trunks. Where part of the cutoff is administrative, cable repair does not shorten that part. |

**How to read §§2.1–2.3.** Each of the three sections has six subsections, in this order. **Definition** defines the level and states what it inherits from the level below. **What the failure looks like** describes what an engineer observes. At the three levels in turn, the engineer observes a brownout (slow, lossy service), a hard edge (international traffic stops outright), and a split between north and south. **Course over time** follows the disruption through numbered Phases, from onset to the slow final recovery.

**Recognition signatures** is the chapter's main diagnostic content: it gives the telemetry (what the measurements show) for each row of the table above. The subsection also shows how to tell the level from the neighboring levels. Where one level has forms that fail by different mechanisms, it shows how to tell those forms apart.

**Simulated scenarios** gives each simulated scenario a block with four parts. The parts are what happens, the precedent, how to tell the simulated scenario from a named one at the same level, and a note for drills. **In Chapter 3** names, in one sentence, where Chapter 3 traces the shared dependencies of the level.

Each block also grades how far its precedent applies to Taiwan. The grade follows the label **Applies to Taiwan:** in the precedent part of the block. The grade *directly* means that the same event could happen in Taiwan. The grade *partly* means that the mechanism applies, but the scale or the conditions differ. The grade *in mechanism only* means that no comparable event exists, so only the mechanism applies.

**Simulated-scenario roster.** A simulated scenario is a concrete, narrowed variant of one level, used for analysis or drills, and it never adds a level to the escalation ladder. The ten simulated scenarios, analyzed in §§2.1–2.3, are:
