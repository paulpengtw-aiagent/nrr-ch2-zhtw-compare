## 2.0 The disruption ladder

This chapter defines an escalation ladder of three disruption scenarios: **50% Degradation** (降載), **Global Cutoff**, and **Island Partition**. Each rung includes every effect of the rung below it. Global Cutoff takes the conditions of 50% Degradation and removes the remaining foreign-routed bandwidth; Island Partition adds the severance of north–south domestic connectivity to that total global loss. The domestic network therefore operates normally on the first two rungs, and Island Partition is the only rung at which domestic inter-city traffic fails. The report anchors its analysis on 50% Degradation: every shared-dependency walk in Chapter 3 begins at that rung and ascends through Global Cutoff to Island Partition.

The table condenses the expected signal on four observable axes; the Recognition signatures slot of each scenario section gives the evidence behind each cell.

| | 50% Degradation | Global Cutoff | Island Partition |
|---|---|---|---|
| BGP behavior | Control plane silent through the sustained plateau (no WITHDRAW messages) while the data plane degrades; Multi-Cable Loss onset adds a transient WITHDRAW spike before reconvergence onto surviving paths | Three patterns, one data-plane outcome: WITHDRAW cliff (physical cable loss); routes advertised, traffic zero (administrative allowlisting, Iran 2026 form); routes advertised, traffic zero (carrier-internal forwarding failure, Rogers 2022 form) | Intra-partition routes stable; cross-partition prefixes withdrawn or unreachable; AS-path geometry shifts toward a new upstream AS |
| Traffic plane | Degraded but surviving; elevated RTT and sustained packet loss; no cliff; domestic network intact | Cliff-edge drop to near zero within minutes of onset; domestic traffic intact | Intra-partition traffic normal; cross-partition and global traffic zero |
| DNS/cache behavior | Gradual SERVFAIL rise as resolver RTT exceeds client timeouts; cache hit rate may rise; no immediate cliff | Immediate SERVFAIL for foreign-authoritative domains when BGP-triggered; cached responses expire in staggered waves: CDN records hours; platform records 2–3 days; long-TTL MX and NS records up to 7 days | Intra-partition resolution intact; cross-partition lookups fail; cache provides a shrinking grace window until TTLs expire |
| Recovery path | Repair-ship queue; weeks–months per cable segment; engineering schedule governs | Physical loss (All-Cable Loss): repair-ship queue governs; weeks per segment. Administrative order with cables intact (Administrative Cutoff): political reversal only; no engineering schedule; range from 7 days ([OONI, November 2019](https://ooni.org/post/2019-iran-internet-blackout/)) to ongoing and record-breaking in April 2026 reporting ([Al Jazeera, April 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/5/frustration-grows-as-irans-wartime-internet-shutdown-breaks-grim-record/)) | Global restoration plus domestic trunk repair (dual-track); no cable-repair shortcut for an administrative-cutoff component |

**How to read §§2.1–2.3.** Each scenario section has six slots in a fixed order. **Definition** states the rung's definition and what carries forward from the rung below. **Failure texture** describes what an engineer observes during the rung: brownout, hard edge, or a split between north and south. **Time texture** follows the rung in prose, in numbered Phases, from onset toward the long tail. **Recognition signatures** gives the telemetry fingerprint on each axis of the table above, with discriminators against the neighboring rungs and between mechanically distinct forms of the same rung; it is the chapter's principal diagnostic content. **Simulated scenarios** gives each canonical simulated scenario a block with its narrative shape, precedent, recognition discriminator against a named sibling, and drill note. **Ch3 pointer** states in one sentence where Chapter 3 walks the rung's shared dependencies.

**Simulated-scenario roster.** A simulated scenario is a concrete, narrowed variant of one rung, used for analysis or drills; it never adds a rung to the ladder. The ten canonical simulated scenarios, analyzed in §§2.1–2.3, are:

| Rung | Canonical simulated scenarios |
|---|---|
| 50% Degradation | Multi-Cable Loss · Gateway Throttle · Compromised Transit |
| Global Cutoff | Administrative Cutoff · All-Cable Loss · Satellite Trickle · Protocol-Clock Cascade |
| Island Partition | Partial-Access Partition · Single-City Isolation · Site-Level Blackout |
