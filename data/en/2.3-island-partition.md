## 2.3 Island Partition

### Definition

Island Partition is the compound worst case on the disruption ladder: the north–south domestic long-haul trunks are severed, so that northern Taiwan cannot reach southern Taiwan, and global connectivity is fully lost at the same time. By escalation inclusion, every effect of Global Cutoff carries forward at full intensity: complete cessation of international traffic, DNS cache decay for non-.tw names, protocol-clock depletion, and hard-edge traffic-plane failure. The brownout effects of 50% Degradation are subsumed and exceeded, and the domestic split is added on top of the global loss.

Each geographic half therefore forms an internally connected network that can reach no other network, domestic or international. The definition requires both total global loss and total north–south severance. A partition in which some global access survives falls outside it and is analyzed only as the simulated scenario Partial-Access Partition.

### Failure texture

Within each half the domestic network runs at its pre-event baseline, while across the partition boundary it fails completely. Inside a half, latency and packet loss are normal and services hosted in the same half are fully reachable. Across the boundary, the failure is hard unreachability rather than degradation: a service in the opposite half is unreachable by the same mechanism as any foreign-hosted service under Global Cutoff. The TCP SYN receives no reply, and the connection attempt ends at the operating system's timeout instead of failing fast or completing slowly.

This cross-partition failure separates Island Partition from both lower rungs. Under 50% Degradation, cross-island traffic is degraded but non-zero; under Global Cutoff, the domestic network is fully operational. Under Island Partition, cross-partition domestic traffic is as unreachable as international traffic.

The primary available analog at sub-national scale is the occupied-territory routing of Kherson, Ukraine, in 2022–2024 (§2.1, Compromised Transit): after Russian forces coerced KhersonTelecom (AS47598) onto Crimean upstreams in May 2022, route-collector data showed the city's isolation from Ukrainian networks ([Kentik, Rerouting of Kherson follows familiar gameplan, August 2022](https://www.kentik.com/blog/rerouting-of-kherson-follows-familiar-gameplan/)), and RIPE Labs measured the matching reversal in latency direction ([RIPE Labs, Resilience of the Internet in Ukraine, One Year On, April 2023](https://labs.ripe.net/author/emileaben/the-resilience-of-the-internet-in-ukraine-one-year-on/)).

Kherson is a structural analog, not a direct precedent. It occurred at sub-national scale, and the non-occupied parts of Ukraine kept their global connectivity. No comparable real-world event documents the pattern that Island Partition requires: a partition at island scale combined with a simultaneous global cutoff.

### Time texture

*(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition)* The partition appears in the routing plane within minutes of trunk severance, as cross-partition BGP prefixes are withdrawn or black-holed.

At Kherson, a city-wide outage on April 30, 2022 was followed on May 1 by the first network's return through the Crimean provider, and after a further outage on May 30 Kherson ISPs lost their links to other Ukrainian networks, "possibly for good"; within a few days every network in the city was either offline or connected through the Crimean provider ([Kentik, Rerouting of Kherson follows familiar gameplan, August 2022](https://www.kentik.com/blog/rerouting-of-kherson-follows-familiar-gameplan/)). *(Inference from the Ukraine/Kherson occupied-territory routing analysis, RIPE Labs and Kentik, 2022–2024; not independently confirmed)* A crystallization window of comparable, days-long scale is the operative planning assumption for a Taiwan partition.

*(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition and the Global Cutoff escalation-inclusion property)* In the first Phase, from hours to days after onset, the Global Cutoff processes run in both halves at once: applications whose servers sit in the opposite half show the Global Cutoff timeout signature, DNS caches for cross-partition and off-island names decay as TTLs expire with no re-fill path, and the protocol-clock depletion already under way (TLS certificate renewal windows, OCSP stapling refresh, DNSSEC RRSIG validity periods) compounds toward the deferred-failure wave described under Protocol-Clock Cascade.

*(Inference from the Ukraine/Kherson occupied-territory routing analysis, RIPE Labs and Kentik, 2022–2024; not independently confirmed)* In a second Phase, a partition maintained by forced rerouting remains sensitive to secondary disruption along the coerced upstream. Downstream of Kherson's forced rerouting, damage to the Kerch Strait Bridge cable in October 2022 raised latency to UMLC-dependent networks by a documented 5–10 ms ([RIPE Labs, Resilience of the Internet in Ukraine, One Year On, April 2023](https://labs.ripe.net/author/emileaben/the-resilience-of-the-internet-in-ukraine-one-year-on/)).

### Recognition signatures

**BGP and routing plane.** *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition)* At onset, cross-partition prefixes are withdrawn or black-holed while prefixes within each half stay announced and stable, so a BGP monitor or route collector observing from inside either half records no withdrawal events, and external route collectors see cross-partition ASNs become unreachable from each side in AS-path data. *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition and the Kentik Kherson routing documentation cited above)* Where the partition is produced by forced rerouting rather than route withdrawal, AS-path geometry changes detectably, with prefixes from one half announced via a new upstream AS within days of onset, as at Kherson (§2.1, Compromised Transit; [Kentik, Rerouting of Kherson follows familiar gameplan, August 2022](https://www.kentik.com/blog/rerouting-of-kherson-follows-familiar-gameplan/)). *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition)* The absence of cross-partition withdrawals together with a new upstream AS path on one side is therefore the routing-plane signature of a forced-rerouting partition, in which physical connectivity survives but transits an adversary upstream, as against a route-withdrawal partition, in which the cross-partition paths disappear from the routing table.

**Traffic plane.** *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition)* Traffic volume and loss rate within each half stay at the pre-event baseline, and cross-partition traffic is zero rather than lossy, failing by timeout as international traffic does under Global Cutoff. *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition)* A RIPE Atlas probe in the northern half reaches northern-half services at baseline latency and times out on all southern-half and international destinations, and a probe in the southern half shows the mirror image. *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition)* This bidirectional split, with normal traffic inside each half, is the definitive telemetry signature distinguishing Island Partition from Global Cutoff, under which probes anywhere on the island see zero outbound connectivity while domestic traffic flows across the whole island. *(Inference from the Ukraine/Kherson occupied-territory routing analysis, RIPE Labs and Kentik, 2022–2024; not independently confirmed)* Because both halves have lost global access, Cloudflare Radar aggregate traffic under baseline Island Partition shows a near-zero profile similar to the Global Cutoff cliff, and separating the two rungs on Cloudflare Radar requires city-level data in which some cities show domestic traffic at baseline alongside zero international traffic, a pattern absent under Global Cutoff, where all cities show near-zero outbound traffic.

**DNS and cache behavior.** *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition and the Global Cutoff escalation-inclusion property)* Within each half, resolution of off-island and cross-partition names follows the Global Cutoff pattern: caches decay as TTLs expire with no re-fill path, and SERVFAIL is returned for any name whose authoritative server is off-island or in the opposite half. *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition)* For a .tw zone whose primary authoritative nameservers sit in the opposite half, secondary nameservers in the near half are the only mechanism that prevents split-brain SERVFAIL, and without them the connectivity failure becomes a name-resolution failure for users in the near half even when the domain's web servers are in that half and operational.

**Recovery path.** *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition)* Recovery requires two failure conditions to be resolved independently, trunk severance on a physical engineering repair timeline and global connectivity loss on a cable-repair or administrative-reversal timeline, and because neither resolves the other, the longest timeline bounds restoration, which gives Island Partition the compound worst-case recovery on the ladder. *(Inference from the Ukraine/Kherson occupied-territory routing analysis, RIPE Labs and Kentik, 2022–2024; not independently confirmed)* Where the partition is maintained by forced rerouting, restoring the pre-partition path requires removal of the coercive external condition, a political or military event rather than a cable splice: at Kherson, latency returned to pre-partition levels "pretty quickly" once liberation on November 11, 2022 removed the coercion, consistent with routing returning to its pre-partition geometry as soon as the AS-path coercion ended ([RIPE Labs, Resilience of the Internet in Ukraine, One Year On, April 2023](https://labs.ripe.net/author/emileaben/the-resilience-of-the-internet-in-ukraine-one-year-on/)).

**Discriminators within the rung.** *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition and the Partial-Access Partition simulated-scenario definition)* Partial-Access Partition departs from the baseline on traffic volume and geographic distribution: where the baseline shows near-zero aggregate traffic from both halves on Cloudflare Radar and IODA active probing, Partial-Access Partition shows a step-down rather than a cliff, and residual traffic from one half gives city-level data an asymmetric geographic profile. *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition and the Single-City Isolation simulated-scenario definition)* Single-City Isolation departs on the geographic scope of the traffic-plane failure: under the baseline, RIPE Atlas probes anywhere in either half show zero cross-partition and zero international reachability, whereas under Single-City Isolation probes outside the isolated city show no partition anomaly and a probe inside it reaches no out-of-city destination. *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition and the Site-Level Blackout simulated-scenario definition)* Site-Level Blackout produces no BGP routing-plane signal and requires no island-wide routing event: a site without upstream connectivity does not participate in the routing table, is indistinguishable in measurement from a powered-off network segment, and shows no split-reachability profile, so its absence from active-probing responses is the only external indicator.

### Simulated scenarios

#### Partial-Access Partition

A simulated scenario under Island Partition: the north–south domestic trunks are severed and global connectivity is largely lost, but one geographic half retains a surviving international cable path that provides limited global egress to that half only, while the other half has none.

**Narrative shape.** The two halves fail asymmetrically. The half without surviving cable access follows the Island Partition baseline, with no cross-partition domestic reachability and no global egress. In the other half, a surviving cable landing station in one geographic cluster, such as the southern cluster at Fangshan (枋山), provides a trickle of global bandwidth. *(Structural inference from the k1, k2, and k3 gateway-kind descriptions in §3.2.1 and the Island Partition rung definition)* Whether that trickle is usable depends on the failure kind of the gateway serving it:

| Gateway kind (§3.2.1) | Global egress of the surviving half |
|---|---|
| k1, administrative shutdown | None in practice: the gateway blocks all egress regardless of the physical path traffic arrives on, so the circuit's physical survival is operationally irrelevant |
| k2, throughput ceiling | Brownout-textured: an administrative throttle on general civilian traffic gives the surviving half something closer to the 50% Degradation failure texture on the international axis, while the other half has no global egress |
| k3, forced rerouting | Traffic flows but transits adversary-controlled infrastructure, which exposes the half's international communications to content inspection and forced path selection |

Under every gateway kind, cross-partition domestic traffic remains unreachable, because the severed north–south trunks are the defining failure of the rung and carry no traffic regardless of gateway policy.

**Precedent.** *(Inference from the Ukraine/Kherson occupied-territory routing analysis, RIPE Labs and Kentik, 2022–2024; not independently confirmed)* The Kherson routing-isolation events document asymmetric access at sub-national scale: Kherson received limited connectivity via Crimea while non-occupied Ukrainian regions kept their own international paths, so two geographically distinct zones with different upstream providers operated simultaneously, and [Kentik, Rerouting of Kherson follows familiar gameplan, August 2022](https://www.kentik.com/blog/rerouting-of-kherson-follows-familiar-gameplan/) and [RIPE Labs, Resilience of the Internet in Ukraine, One Year On, April 2023](https://labs.ripe.net/author/emileaben/the-resilience-of-the-internet-in-ukraine-one-year-on/) document the difference in routing geometry and the asymmetry in latency direction between them. *(Inference from the dComms roundtable, June 2026; not independently confirmed)* The dComms roundtable identified the gateway failure kind (k1, k2, or k3) as the determinant of whether a surviving physical cable yields usable global egress. Taiwan transferability: structural. Because Taiwan's southern landing stations at Fangshan (枋山) are geographically separated from the northern stations, asymmetric physical survival of one station cluster is plausible under the physical triggers that produce Island Partition.

**Recognition discriminator.** *(Structural inference from the Partial-Access Partition and Single-City Isolation simulated-scenario definitions)* Versus Single-City Isolation, on geographic scope: Partial-Access Partition involves island-wide north–south trunk severance with asymmetric international access, whereas Single-City Isolation involves one municipality losing all upstream links while the domestic network in the rest of each half remains intact.

**Drill note.** A tabletop exercise for this scenario would vary the surviving half's gateway failure kind across runs (k1 administratively dark, k2 throttled, k3 adversary-surveilled, or open) and ask cross-functional teams, without access to out-of-scenario network information, which services and communication paths survive under each gateway condition.

#### Single-City Isolation

A simulated scenario under Island Partition: one city, a municipality or urban area, loses all upstream links to the rest of the island's domestic network simultaneously, while the broader island network continues to operate within each half of the partition.

**Narrative shape.** A single physical event severs every upstream ingress link serving the city: a trunk failure along a geographic corridor, cable damage at a bridge or mountain pass, or the simultaneous loss of all last-mile aggregation points feeding the city. The domestic network elsewhere in each half continues to operate normally. From inside the city, every service hosted outside city boundaries is unreachable, including services on the rest of the island, which the rung's north–south partition already divides and the city's own upstream failure now cuts off entirely, and international services, which the concurrent Global Cutoff already removes. Services hosted within the city continue to serve local users on the city's internal network. Emergency responders in the city can therefore reach only city-local resources; coordination systems, databases, and communication paths hosted anywhere outside the city are as unreachable as foreign ones.

**Precedent.** *(Inference from the Ukraine/Kherson occupied-territory routing analysis, RIPE Labs and Kentik, 2022–2024; not independently confirmed)* The Kherson case documents routing isolation at city scale: all of Kherson's internet access was rerouted through a single non-Ukrainian upstream, which made the city a routing zone disconnected from the Ukrainian domestic network, and [Kentik, Rerouting of Kherson follows familiar gameplan, August 2022](https://www.kentik.com/blog/rerouting-of-kherson-follows-familiar-gameplan/) documents the BGP path change that produced this isolation. *(Inference from the dComms roundtable, June 2026; not independently confirmed)* The dComms roundtable identified Single-City Isolation as a drill-relevant scenario for civil-society and private-sector engineers reasoning about local service survival without out-of-city coordination. Taiwan transferability: the scenario requires only the simultaneous severance of a city's upstream long-haul fiber routes, which typically enter through a small number of trunk corridors and terrain chokepoints, and such severance is structurally plausible under the physical triggers that produce Island Partition.

**Recognition discriminator.** *(Structural inference from the Partial-Access Partition and Single-City Isolation simulated-scenario definitions)* Versus Partial-Access Partition, on the extent of the domestic failure: Single-City Isolation does not require island-wide north–south trunk severance and can co-occur with intact domestic connectivity in every non-isolated part of each half, so its failure is confined to one municipality.

**Drill note.** A tabletop exercise for this scenario would restrict participants to a single-city information environment, in which only resources and communication paths physically present within city boundaries are accessible, and ask teams which locally hosted services, local coordination mechanisms, and local human-organizational paths remain functional when out-of-city network access is zero.

#### Site-Level Blackout

A simulated scenario under Island Partition: a single rescue, shelter, or critical-infrastructure site has no upstream connectivity whatsoever, because its last-mile links are completely severed, leaving it an isolated on-site network with no path to any external node on the domestic network or the global internet.

**Narrative shape.** The site's last-mile connections to all upstream providers are severed simultaneously by cut fiber, destroyed antenna installations, or loss of the power supply that sustains its radio links. The site's internal network continues to operate: devices reach each other, and locally hosted services and locally cached data remain accessible. No packet crosses the site boundary in either direction. Staff cannot reach emergency coordination systems hosted elsewhere, verify instructions from external authorities, transmit status reports, or access any live data source outside the site. Every digital resource that the site depended on but did not host, such as patient records, authorization systems, real-time operational data, and software updates, is unreachable, and the site's only working network resources are those that were present and operational on site before the last-mile link failed.

**Precedent.** *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition and the site-connectivity premise of the simulated scenario)* Site-Level Blackout is entailed by the Island Partition rung definition: if partition and global cutoff together sever all paths external to each half, a site whose last-mile links are also severed within that half has no path to any external node by construction. No primary source documents an event at exactly this scale, a single rescue or shelter site with complete last-mile severance under simultaneous Island Partition conditions, so the precedent is grounded in mechanism rather than incident. The war in Ukraine offers a partial illustration at comparable granularity: individual Starlink terminals were the sole connectivity path for some frontline sites, and their loss or suppression left those sites with no electronic communication path to any outside node ([Cloudflare Blog, One Year of War in Ukraine, February 2023](https://blog.cloudflare.com/one-year-of-war-in-ukraine/)). Taiwan transferability: direct. Any critical site in Taiwan that depends on a single last-mile connection path is susceptible to this pattern under the physical conditions that produce Island Partition.

**Recognition discriminator.** *(Structural inference from the Island Partition rung definition and the Site-Level Blackout simulated-scenario definition)* Versus Single-City Isolation, on the BGP and routing-plane axis: Single-City Isolation produces a routing-plane event for the city's upstream AS paths, observable in route-collector data, and a probe-visible zero-reachability signature from within the city's address space, whereas Site-Level Blackout produces neither, because a site with no upstream connectivity does not participate in the routing table.

**Drill note.** A tabletop exercise for this scenario would give responders a single-site information environment, with no external network access and only locally cached data and local communication paths, and ask which operational decisions can be made from local resources alone, which require out-of-site coordination that is unreachable, and which resources would need to be present at the site before the last-mile link failed.
