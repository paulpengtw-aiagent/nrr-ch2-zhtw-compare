## 2.1 50% Degradation

### Definition

50% Degradation is the first rung of the disruption ladder: the whole island loses approximately 50% of its bandwidth to the global internet, and the domestic network remains intact. That network comprises users, internet service providers (ISPs), domestic internet exchange points (IXPs: TPIX, TWIX, STUIX), and domestically hosted services. The report calls the failure texture of this rung brownout and reserves the name 50% Degradation for the scenario itself.

No rung lies below 50% Degradation, so it inherits no effects. Escalation inclusion therefore runs upward only: every effect described in this section persists under Global Cutoff and Island Partition, whereas the complete loss of international traffic that defines Global Cutoff and the north–south domestic severance that Island Partition adds do not occur at this rung.

### Failure texture

Under brownout, capacity is degraded rather than severed: connections mostly succeed, but slowly and lossily, with sharp failures in the tail. The congestion is confined to international paths, and the contrast is itself diagnostic: because the domestic network remains intact, intra-Taiwan traffic flows normally. At the application layer, requests fail by timeout rather than by refusal. HTTP requests stall and either complete late or time out (HTTP 504, not 502 or 503), TCP sessions survive in degraded form, video buffering stalls, text messages arrive after minute-scale delays, and real-time voice drops intermittently. The timeout mode separates the rung from Global Cutoff, where TCP SYNs receive no reply and connection attempts end at the operating system's hard timeout, and from the undisrupted baseline, where requests complete promptly.

Services fail in an order set by their sensitivity to latency and throughput. Streaming video, voice, and real-time synchronization fail first; text-and-acknowledgement protocols survive longest; and priority traffic such as banking, government, and hospital communications can be ring-fenced on the surviving capacity when quality-of-service (QoS) policy is applied. The order was observed in the Matsu 2023 event, when the severance of both of the island's cables cut capacity by approximately 75%, to roughly 25% of normal throughput: sending a text message could take 15 to 20 minutes, voice calls dropped intermittently, Netflix and Instagram became unwatchable, and Chunghwa Telecom reserved its microwave backup mainly for telephone, national-security, government, aviation, and banking circuits, with preferential bandwidth for government agencies, military units, and hospitals, while the Ministry of Digital Affairs (MODA) offered additional frequency bands to expand the relay ([Taipei Times, February 10, 2023](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2023/02/10/2003794094); [Taipei Times, February 17, 2023](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2023/02/17/2003794526); [Central News Agency via TechNews, February 16, 2023](https://technews.tw/2023/02/16/tw-matsu-cable-cht/); [The Wire China, June 2023](https://www.thewirechina.com/2023/06/11/cable-cutters-matsu-taiwan-china-undersea-internet-cables/); [The Diplomat, April 2023](https://thediplomat.com/2023/04/after-chinese-vessels-cut-matsu-internet-cables-taiwan-shows-its-communications-resilience/)). The microwave backup provided 2.2 Gbps at initial activation, against a normal cable capacity of 8–9 Gbps ([MODA press release, February 17, 2025](https://moda.gov.tw/en/press/press-releases/15210); [Domino Theory, Taiwan Internet Cable Problems](https://dominotheory.com/taiwans-internet-cable-problems-run-deep/)).

### Time texture

Onset is rapid. Cable severance reduces the available international bandwidth within seconds to minutes of the physical break; where a transient Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) reconvergence accompanies the loss, as under Multi-Cable Loss, the routing table then settles onto surviving paths, a process that measurements of inter-domain routing have found can take up to tens of minutes, with intermittent loss and elevated latency until it completes ([Labovitz et al., ACM SIGCOMM, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/347059.347428)). *(Structural inference from the Matsu 2023 capacity ratio of approximately 25% of normal and the application-failure-order pattern documented in the Failure texture above)* In the first Phase, full-island demand immediately saturates the surviving international bandwidth and the failure order sets in: streaming and voice collapse as per-user throughput falls below encoding and jitter thresholds, and priority allocation, if invoked, takes effect within the same Phase.

The degraded plateau then lasts until its cause is removed. In the Matsu 2023 event it lasted 51 days, from the second cable cut on approximately February 8, 2023, to the first partial restoration on March 31, 2023. Microwave capacity was raised incrementally: to 3.8 Gbps by March 6, 2023, to cable-equivalent capacity by the end of 2023, and to 12.6 Gbps by January 2025, above the weekday peak demand of 9.5 Gbps ([Taipei Times, March 8, 2023](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2023/03/08/2003795708); [MODA press release, February 17, 2025](https://moda.gov.tw/en/press/press-releases/15210)).

For physical cable damage, the repair-ship queue sets the length of the plateau. *(Structural inference from TeleGeography cable repair fleet size data)* Because the global repair-ship fleet of approximately 60 vessels (fewer than 20 dedicated solely to repair) serves approximately 150–200 cable faults a year, a simultaneous multi-cable event in Taiwan draws on a queue shared with the entire global cable plant ([Scientific American, citing TeleGeography fleet data](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/iran-threats-expose-the-aging-fleet-that-repairs-undersea-internet-cables/)). Access restrictions can extend the queue by months beyond the technical minimum: in the Red Sea in 2024, repair vessels needed permits negotiated with Houthi-faction and Yemeni government actors before they could enter the repair zone ([GeoCables, 2024](https://geocables.com/research/red-sea-cable-cuts-2024); [Marine Insight, Yemen political delay, 2024](https://www.marineinsight.com/shipping-news/yemens-political-disputes-delay-full-repairs-of-3-submarine-internet-cables-in-the-red-sea/)). Damage to cables on Taiwan's eastern seabed in contested waters could meet the same access delay. A Gateway Throttle or Compromised Transit plateau has no physical component, so the queue does not bound it.

### Recognition signatures

**BGP and routing plane.** *(Structural inference from RFC 4271 route-withdrawal mechanics and the rung's definition as capacity-constrained rather than route-severing)* No WITHDRAW messages are issued during the sustained brownout plateau, because surviving cable routes remain active in the routing table and the bottleneck is physical capacity rather than reachability ([IETF RFC 4271, January 2006](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4271)). BGP update volume over the plateau stays flat or rises marginally from edge-link flapping, without the multi-standard-deviation spike that marks Global Cutoff onset, so BGP-only monitoring tools raise no alert. The primary fingerprint of the rung is this combination of a silent control plane with a degraded data plane (elevated round-trip time, or RTT; elevated packet loss; reduced throughput). It separates 50% Degradation from Global Cutoff, where the control and data planes both signal disruption, and from the undisrupted baseline, where neither does. No BGP event record was published for Matsu 2023: OONI does not probe Taiwan's outlying-island link to Matsu, because its focus is international censorship and accessibility rather than inter-island cable quality, and IODA active probing is oriented to international reachability, not to intra-Taiwan domestic cables ([OONI methodology, ooni.org/docs/methodology]; [IODA platform scope, ioda.inetintel.cc.gatech.edu]).

**Traffic plane.** *(Structural inference from the rung's capacity-constrained definition and the Matsu 2023 measured capacity ratio of approximately 25% of normal)* Cloudflare Radar traffic shows a sustained depression over the degraded period, with a slope-down, plateau, and slope-up shape rather than the near-vertical drop to near zero that marks Global Cutoff onset. IODA active probes to the affected address space keep responding, with elevated RTT and elevated loss. *(Structural inference from TCP congestion-window mechanics per RFC 5681 and the rung's capacity-constrained definition)* Delivered throughput falls below the surviving-capacity fraction, because sustained packet loss triggers congestion-window reduction in TCP slow-start and congestion avoidance ([IETF RFC 5681, September 2009](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5681)).

**DNS and cache behavior.** *(Structural inference from RFC 1034 recursive-resolver timeout mechanics and the rung's capacity-constrained definition)* Name resolution degrades gradually rather than at a cliff: the SERVFAIL rate (the response code a resolver returns when it cannot complete a lookup) rises as resolver RTT exceeds client timeouts, the NXDOMAIN rate is unchanged because authoritative servers remain reachable, if slowly, and the cache hit rate may rise as clients retry local caches, which partially masks the degradation; Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP) stapling typically succeeds where staples were refreshed shortly before onset, and Transport Layer Security (TLS) handshakes mostly complete at elevated latency, with sharp failures in the p99 tail ([IETF RFC 1034, November 1987](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1034)). Controlled experiments support the gradual texture: with 50% packet loss at the authoritative servers, caching and retries let almost all clients still resolve names, at the cost of higher tail latency ([Moura et al., ACM IMC, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3278532.3278534)).

**Recovery path.** The cause determines how capacity returns. Under Multi-Cable Loss, capacity returns incrementally as each segment is repaired, at weeks per segment. Under Gateway Throttle, the cable plant is intact throughout and recovery is a policy decision with no engineering schedule. Under Compromised Transit, recovery requires a change of routing control upstream; neither cable repair nor a policy reversal at the Taiwan gateway level resolves it.

**Sub-variant discriminators within the rung.** The three simulated scenarios of this rung differ in two routing-plane signals recorded by route-collector feeds such as RIPE RIS and Routeviews:

| | Multi-Cable Loss | Gateway Throttle | Compromised Transit |
|---|---|---|---|
| WITHDRAW at onset | Transient spike, then silence after reconvergence onto surviving paths | None: only the traffic-classification layer changes | None: coerced rerouting substitutes a new upstream AS without withdrawing routes |
| AS-path change | None: surviving routes keep their original peering relationships | None | Adversary-controlled transit ASNs replace neutral carrier ASNs |

### Simulated scenarios

#### Multi-Cable Loss

A simulated scenario under 50% Degradation: one physical event severs several international cable systems at once; the surviving subset carries brownout-textured load, and the global repair-ship queue stretches restoration far beyond the per-segment baseline.

**Narrative shape.** A seismic event, a submarine landslide, or coordinated damage cuts several cable systems in rapid succession. The surviving cable plant absorbs the rerouted load at reduced aggregate international bandwidth, and all users sharing the remaining capacity experience brownout. Landing-station loss with partial reroute is a folded variant of this simulated scenario: physical damage takes one or more cable landing stations out of service while the others keep operating, cables landing at the surviving stations absorb the lost station's traffic share, and the depth of the brownout is proportional to the share of traffic that had passed through the damaged station.

**Precedent.** The 2006 Hengchun earthquake (December 26; two shocks of M7.0 and M6.9 off southern Taiwan) triggered submarine landslides that severed eight cable systems: APCN, APCN-2, C2C, China-US CN, EAC, FLAG FEA, FNAL/RNAL, and SMW3. Repair required 11 ships and 49 days ([Submarine Networks, December 2006](https://www.submarinenetworks.com/en/nv/news/cables-cut-after-taiwan-earthquake-2006)). At the end of 2025, earthquake damage off eastern Taiwan disrupted six international cables (SJC2, PLCN, EAC1, FNAL, RNAL, and EAC2); MODA reported that all operators had completed traffic rerouting and described external communication as stable, with possible slight delays at peak hours ([MODA press release, December 2025](https://moda.gov.tw/en/press/press-releases/18412); [MODA press release, April 2026](https://moda.gov.tw/en/press/press-releases/19392)). Taiwan transferability: direct. Hengchun is a main-island precedent at worst-case simultaneous-damage scale, and the end-2025 response shows MODA rerouting in operation at current cable-plant scale for the multi-cable shape.

**Recognition discriminator.** Versus Gateway Throttle, on BGP update activity: Multi-Cable Loss produces a transient WITHDRAW spike in route-collector feeds at onset, as routes through the severed cables are withdrawn before traffic reconverges onto surviving paths, and then settles into BGP silence; Gateway Throttle produces no BGP update activity in any Phase, because only the traffic-classification policy at the deep-packet-inspection (DPI) layer changes.

**Drill note.** A tabletop exercise that gives participants a pre-scripted BGP reconvergence log, a surviving-capacity ratio drawn from the eight-system Hengchun scale, and a repair-queue timeline lengthened by access-permit delay would test whether teams identify the brownout signal and distinguish it from Global Cutoff onset.

#### Gateway Throttle

A simulated scenario under 50% Degradation: the cables are intact, but international gateways enforce an administrative throttle on general traffic (the k2 gateway kind of §3.2.1); the degradation is uniform across carriers and shows neither a cable fault nor a route-withdrawal signature.

**Narrative shape.** A government or carrier operator instructs gateway routers to hold general civilian traffic under a throughput ceiling by DPI-based traffic classification, while designated critical or whitelisted services keep their bandwidth. At the user and application layers the resulting brownout is indistinguishable from a cable-loss brownout.

**Precedent.** On March 10, 2021, Roskomnadzor ordered Russian ISPs to throttle Twitter through TSPU DPI hardware. Censored Planet, probing actively from inside Russia, independently measured a ceiling of 130–150 kbps on 100% of mobile and 50% of fixed-line devices; the throttle persisted on mobile through at least May 2021, and the peer-reviewed IMC 2021 paper describes it as "the first ever use of large-scale, targeted throttling for censorship purposes" ([Censored Planet / IMC 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3487552.3487858); [Access Now tracker](https://www.accessnow.org/russia-throttled-twitter/)). Taiwan transferability: moderate. Taiwan has no sovereign-internet infrastructure or TSPU-equivalent hardware in place, so the mechanism would require emergency regulatory authorization and carrier-level DPI hardware, which is standard equipment in major ISPs; the simulated scenario rests on demonstrated technical feasibility, not on an institutional precedent analogous to Taiwan's regulatory environment.

**Recognition discriminator.** Versus Compromised Transit, on the BGP AS-path axis: under Gateway Throttle the routing topology is unchanged, because the throttle acts at the traffic-classification layer, which BGP route collectors do not see; under Compromised Transit, adversary-controlled transit ASNs displace neutral carrier ASNs on the affected international routes in route-collector feeds.

**Drill note.** A tabletop exercise for carrier-operations and product-engineering participants, given DPI-filtered traffic metrics and no BGP alert signals, would test whether teams recognize the k2 gateway-policy brownout and distinguish it from cable-loss onset without any routing-plane evidence.

#### Compromised Transit

A simulated scenario under 50% Degradation: international capacity survives but is coerced through adversary-controlled upstream infrastructure (the k3 gateway kind of §3.2.1, on the Kherson pattern); measured by bandwidth it reads as brownout, but its defining texture is interception exposure and chronic instability.

**Narrative shape.** Gateway operators are coerced, physically or administratively, into rerouting international traffic through adversary-controlled upstream ASNs. Connectivity survives: traffic reaches foreign destinations, and the BGP routes remain technically valid. All of that traffic, however, crosses adversary-controlled infrastructure, where it is exposed to content inspection, censorship, and forced BGP path selection. At Kherson, the instability took the form of a near-total outage on July 26, 2022, approximately two months after the initial coerced routing change, followed by a connection that remained unstable for more than a year ([Freedom House, Freedom on the Net 2022 Ukraine](https://freedomhouse.org/country/ukraine/freedom-net/2022)).

**Precedent.** In May 2022, Russian occupying forces in Kherson, Ukraine, coerced local ISP staff into shifting their BGP upstreams to Miranda-Media (AS201776) in Crimea and onward to Rostelecom; the network owner stated that they "had no choice", and Kentik's BGP analysis confirms the AS-path change ([Kentik, Rerouting of Kherson follows familiar gameplan, August 2022](https://www.kentik.com/blog/rerouting-of-kherson-follows-familiar-gameplan/)). RIPE Labs measurements show that after the rerouting, latency toward western networks rose and latency toward Russian networks fell, and that both reverted to the pre-occupation geometry immediately after liberation in November 2022 ([RIPE Labs, Resilience of the Internet in Ukraine, One Year On, April 2023](https://labs.ripe.net/author/emileaben/the-resilience-of-the-internet-in-ukraine-one-year-on/)). Taiwan transferability: structural analog. The routing-capture technique is mechanically identical to the Kherson precedent and applies to any ISP whose physical gateway an adversary can access; the geographic and political conditions differ.

**Recognition discriminator.** Versus Multi-Cable Loss, on the BGP AS-path axis: under Compromised Transit, adversary-controlled transit ASNs appear in the AS paths of affected international routes where neutral carrier ASNs previously appeared, visibly in RIPE RIS or Routeviews route-collector feeds; under Multi-Cable Loss the AS paths of surviving routes do not change, because surviving cables carry traffic through their original peering relationships and the loss is one of capacity, not of routing topology.

**Drill note.** A tabletop exercise that gives an engineering team route-collector AS-path data showing adversary transit ASNs, together with degraded throughput and content-accessibility metrics but no cable-fault evidence or BGP-withdrawal signal, would test whether the team identifies the interception-exposure texture of Compromised Transit and distinguishes it from a capacity-only brownout.
